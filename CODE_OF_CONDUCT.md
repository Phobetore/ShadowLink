# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help. Argue about the code, not
about the person writing it.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism about the work. "This breaks the deletion guarantee because X"
  is useful; "this is amateur" is not.
- Accept that a change can be declined. Maintaining something means saying no to
  work that is perfectly good but wrong for the project, and that is not a
  judgement about you.
- Give people room to be wrong. Nobody arrives knowing why the reconciler
  recomputes everything instead of patching.
- Remember there is a person on the other end, usually in a different timezone,
  doing this outside a job.

## What is not

Harassment of any kind, personal attacks, discrimination on any ground,
deliberately derailing discussions, publishing someone's private information, or
persistently pushing a change after a considered decision has been made.

Reporting a vulnerability publicly instead of through
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) also belongs here: it puts real users at risk to make
a point.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and anywhere else the
project is represented.

## Reporting

Email **broucewayn1413@protonmail.com**, or use GitHub's private reporting on the
repository. Reports go to the maintainer only.

Say what happened and where. You do not need to have a solution in mind, and you
will not be asked to justify being uncomfortable.

Expect an acknowledgement within a week. Depending on what happened, the response
ranges from a private word to a permanent ban. The maintainer decides, and will
tell you what was done.

## A note on scale

This project is one person and, so far, a handful of contributors. There is no
committee and no appeals process — if that matters to you, it is better to know
now. What there is, is someone who will read what you send and take it seriously.
